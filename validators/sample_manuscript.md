# A Retrospective Cohort Study of HFpEF Outcomes Using LVEF and NT-proBNP for the Comprehensive Prediction of Rehospitalization.

## Abstract
Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is common. In this retrospective cohort study we analyzed 812 hospitalized patients to evaluate whether baseline LVEF and NT-proBNP predict rehospitalization within twelve months of discharge from a single tertiary center between 2018 and 2023. Treated patients had higher LVEF than controls, with a median of 47.5 versus 37.5, and the difference reached statistical significance at a p value of 0.0039 as previously reported [12]. The adjusted hazard ratio for rehospitalization was 1.85 in patients with lower ejection fraction. These findings suggest that simple, inexpensive baseline measurements available at admission carry meaningful prognostic information and could be incorporated into routine risk stratification workflows for this population, although prospective validation in multicenter settings is required before any clinical recommendation can be responsibly made by treating physicians.

## Introduction
Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction accounts for roughly half of all heart failure cases and its prognosis remains poor. Prior cohort studies have linked natriuretic peptides to outcomes.

## Results
Among 812 patients, the treated group showed a higher median LVEF than the control group (47.5 vs 37.5; Mann-Whitney p=0.0039). In the adjusted Cox model the hazard ratio for rehospitalization was 2.10 (95% CI 1.40-3.15). Baseline characteristics are summarized in **Table 1**.

## Discussion
Our findings are consistent with prior work. Limitations include the single-center retrospective design and potential residual confounding.

## Methods
This was a retrospective cohort of patients admitted with heart failure. Continuous variables were compared with the Mann-Whitney U test; time-to-event with Cox proportional hazards. Proportional hazards assumptions were checked with Schoenfeld residuals.
